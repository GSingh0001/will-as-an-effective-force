# Will as an Effective Force: Units, Channel Selection Coupling, and a Thermodynamic Floor

**Ganga Singh**
Independent Researcher
ORCID: 0009-0004-2191-356X

## Abstract

This paper proposes that will can be treated as an effective force, in the same sense that friction is a force: universal in form, tabulated in coefficients, and emergent rather than fundamental. The proposal is built to be wrong in checkable ways. Will is defined operationally, once, and held to that definition throughout: the will favouring an outcome is the shift it produces in the log odds of that outcome being selected, measured in nats against an unforced baseline, with zero at indifference and independent pulls adding. Three questions that any force claim must answer are then addressed in turn. First, units: the log-odds unit is tested against four metrological criteria and validated on synthetic data, where a known dilution curve is recovered from choice frequencies alone with a pointwise error of 0.0562 nats. Second, coupling: of four candidate points at which will could couple to physical dynamics, outcome bias and noise steering are excluded by numerical kill tests, measurement-strength modulation turns out to be standard continuous measurement theory under another name, and one coupling survives intact, selection among channels and measurement bases, which leaves the statistics inside any selected channel unchanged. Third, conservation: the currency exchanged when will acts is information. Under a stated Boltzmann implementation, a log-odds shift corresponds to an energy-gap change of W times k_B T, and the minimum dissipation for preparing the biased distribution is bounded below by k_B T times its relative entropy to baseline, an accounting a simulation implements self-consistently to within 0.005759 k_B T. An earlier and stronger form of this claim, a universal charge of k_B T per nat of log-odds shift, is corrected and withdrawn in Section 5. The paper also states its defects without decoration. The effort and time layer fails three of its four unit tests and is demoted to a calibration role. The thermodynamic floor, expressed as a power at one selection per two-second decision trial, sits about 23 orders of magnitude below whole-brain metabolic power and cannot be resolved in a living human. In the choice domain, the dilution law at the centre of the theory is mathematically equivalent to divisive normalisation and loses out of sample to nested logit models, and it is presented there as a renaming, not a result. The falsifier is stated here, in advance: if the dilution law, measured in the person domain, gives no out-of-sample predictive gain over discounted utility combined with standard attention measures, then the force framing fails, the theory drops to a metaphor, and this paper should be read as the record of a precise mistake.

**Keywords:** effective theory; volition; log-odds; channel selection; Landauer bound

## 1. Introduction

Physics is comfortable with forces that are not fundamental. Friction is the standard example. No one expects a friction term in the Lagrangian of the universe, yet the form of the friction law is general, its coefficients are measured system by system, and bridges are designed with it. Temperature has the same status relative to molecular motion: real, measurable, and emergent. This paper asks whether will can be given the same standing, and it accepts the obligations that come with the request. A force claim, even an effective one, owes three things: a unit, a coupling, and a conservation story. A quantity with no unit cannot appear in an equation. An influence with no stated coupling point cannot be tested, because nothing specifies where it acts. An action with no accounting of what is exchanged is not a mechanism but a gesture.

The word will is used loosely in ordinary language and in several technical literatures, and loose use is the first enemy of the programme pursued here. Section 2 therefore fixes one operational definition and holds it throughout. Everything later is either derived from that definition, marked as inserted where it is postulated rather than derived, or recorded as a defect where the construction fails its own tests. The reader should finish the paper knowing exactly what would have to be true for the theory to be right, and exactly which observations would end it.

The programme has a fork at its centre. Decoherence explains why the classical world looks classical: interaction with an environment suppresses interference and selects a preferred basis. What decoherence does not do, by itself, is select the single outcome that is recorded. That selection point is the one structural place where a volitional influence could conceivably enter physics, and a frozen test programme has mapped what any candidate selector is permitted to look like. In every case the programme has constructed and graded, a selector that respects Born frequencies and the linearity of ensemble evolution has taken the form of continuous measurement theory. This produces two horns. On the first, will is identical in structure to continuous measurement, an interpretation of known physics rather than new physics. On the second, will deviates from that form somewhere specific, and the deviation is a measurable prediction that experiment or simulation can kill. This paper works the agent side of the fork: it builds the effective theory where the evidence says it can live, in an agent's selection among channels, and grades the physical candidates honestly where they arise.

The paper's organisation follows the three obligations. Section 3 defines the unit and reports its synthetic validation, including the failure of its calibration layer. Section 4 grades four candidate couplings against three kill tests. Section 5 states the conservation accounting, including a correction of its own earlier and stronger form, and the measurability limit that prevents its floor from being checked in a living brain. Section 6 collects the theory's anatomy in friction form. Section 7 places the theory beside its nearest neighbours, and Section 8 gives the audited status of the claims, including the domains where the theory has already lost. The programme rule throughout is that a component counts as finished when it produces a statement precise enough to be wrong. Where a number comes from a simulation, the simulation is identified. Where a claim has been killed, the killing test is named. Where the theory is indistinguishable from an older theory, the older theory is named and the overlap conceded.

## 2. One operational definition, held throughout

### 2.1 The definition

Let an agent face a choice between a target goal g and competing alternatives in a context C. Let P_0(g) be the baseline probability of selecting g when no directed intention is applied, set by default bias, prior frequency, or chance. Let P(g) be the probability of selecting g when the agent's intention is engaged. The will favouring g is the shift in the natural logarithm of the selection odds:

W(g) = logit(P(g)) minus logit(P_0(g)),

where logit(p) = ln(p / (1 minus p)). The unit is the nat. One nat of will multiplies the odds of selecting g over not selecting g by e, approximately 2.71828, with all baseline affordances held fixed. Zero nats is indifference, meaning selection at the unforced baseline. Positive values are pursuit, negative values are active aversion or resistance. Independent pulls add: the total will on an outcome is the sum of the log-odds shifts contributed by each independent influence. When base-2 logarithms are used instead, the unit is the bit, and 1 nat equals approximately 1.4427 bits.

Three features of this definition do work later. First, it is read off behaviour: choice frequencies are the instrument, and any selecting system that produces frequencies, a person, an animal, a language model emitting tokens, a market placing orders, can in principle be measured with it. Second, it is comparative: will is always a shift against a stated baseline, so every measurement carries its baseline with it, and disputes about the number become disputes about the baseline, which can be argued on evidence. Third, it separates the strength of an intention from its success. A strong will can favour an outcome that does not occur, and a weak will can coincide with one that does. The quantity is the pull, not the result.

Competing pulls inside one agent are settled by comparison of the same quantity. In the theory's original formulation, a goal's undiluted pull is the product of the intensity of its expected gratification and the tenure over which that gratification is expected to persist; Section 6 returns to that form. The agent acts on the goal whose will, after dilution by competing channels, exceeds the combined pull of its rivals. Focus is the regime in which one channel has driven the effective competition down.

### 2.2 What this paper does not mean by will

Two older senses of the word are excluded by name, so that they can never trade places with the operational sense inside an argument. The first is Schopenhauer's Will: a metaphysical, blind, universal striving behind the world of representation. That notion is not individual, not measurable in frequencies, and not what any equation in this paper refers to. It is at most a motivation for finding the measurement problem interesting. Motivation is not a premise, and no derivation below depends on it. The second excluded sense is metaphysical free will, whether choices are ultimately uncaused or compatible with determinism. The operational definition is silent on that question by construction: a deterministic agent and a stochastic agent can both exhibit measurable log-odds shifts, and the theory prices the shifts identically. Readers looking for a verdict on free will will not find one here, because the instrument cannot deliver one.

## 3. Units

### 3.1 Why the unit had to come first

Until a unit exists, no coupling can be written down, because there is nothing to put on the left side of the equation. The unit also carries the theory's main risk. Define will by choices, then explain choices by will, and the construction is a circle: the quantity is a post hoc relabelling of observed frequency, and the theory is a vocabulary, not a claim. The definition in Section 2 escapes the circle only if two conditions hold, stated here as obligations rather than as virtues already possessed. The first is out-of-sample parameter invariance: parameters estimated in one set of choice environments must predict frequencies in new environments, at new load levels, without refitting. The second is cross-instrument transfer: the calibration converting a person's physical effort into nats in one task must predict that person's behaviour in an independent task. These two conditions are the operational content of the falsifier stated in the abstract. The dilution law earns the out-of-sample predictive gain that the falsifier demands only if parameters estimated in one environment hold in new environments and the calibration transfers to independent tasks, so a failure of either condition in the person domain is a failure of the abstract's test. The unit work in this section establishes that the definition is coherent and recoverable under controlled conditions. It cannot establish that human beings satisfy the two conditions. That is an empirical question, and it is still open.

### 3.2 The two-layer architecture

The measurement framework has two layers, with different standings that this paper will not blur. The primary layer is the log-odds unit of Section 2, cross-domain by construction. The secondary layer is a set of person-domain instruments intended to calibrate the primary unit in human subjects: persistence time against a standard competing pull, physical work against resistance, cognitive load tolerance, and monetary sacrifice in titration paradigms of the kind used in effort discounting research. Its status is that of historical thermometers before temperature had a statistical definition: local, fallible calibration devices for a quantity that is not itself local. As Section 3.4 reports, it earns that status the hard way, by failing.

### 3.3 Metrological grading of the primary layer

A unit, to serve as a standard, should pass four tests: an instrument-independent readout, a real zero, a composition rule, and portability across domains. The primary layer passes all four at the level of definition, with a qualification that matters for two of them: readout and portability pass definitionally, because the quantity is extractable wherever choice frequencies exist, while their empirical content, parameter invariance across environments and transfer across instruments, remains an open obligation, as Sections 3.1, 6 and 8 state. Its readout is instrument-independent because the same log-odds shift can be extracted from binary choices, multi-alternative choices, reaction time distributions through the drift rate of a diffusion model, or betting odds. Its zero is real: zero nats is odds ratio one, selection exactly at the unforced baseline. Its composition rule is additivity of independent shifts, the algebraic content of the logit form. Its portability is the point of the definition: frequencies exist wherever selection occurs, so the quantity is defined in every domain, which is a definitional pass and not yet an empirical one.

The failures of the secondary layer are recorded in Section 3.4. Here the defects of the primary layer itself must be recorded, because a unit with unrecorded failure modes is a liability to every later section.

The first defect is boundary saturation. In any finite experiment with M trials, an agent that selects the target on every trial yields an empirical frequency of one, whose logit is infinite. A finite-sample correction, such as the Haldane-Anscombe correction of adding half a count to each cell, keeps the estimate finite at the price of a ceiling: the maximum measurable will is approximately ln(2M) nats. Any pull stronger than that cannot be resolved by an instrument running M trials. Strong will, on this unit, is literally harder to measure than weak will, and the ceiling rises only logarithmically with experimental effort.

The second defect is contextual non-stationarity. Mass and charge do not drift with time of day; human motivational state does, with circadian rhythm, metabolic state, and ambient stress, so the calibration coefficients connecting effort to nats are non-stationary over multi-hour scales. A measurement of a person's will is a measurement of that person in that state, and the theory treats the state dependence as part of the system-specific coefficients of Section 6 rather than as noise. Whether that treatment is legitimate is itself testable, through the transfer condition of Section 3.1.

The third defect is that additivity rests on the independence of irrelevant alternatives. Real human choice exhibits context, decoy, and similarity effects, in which a third option changes the odds between two others in ways a scalar field cannot express. Where those effects are large, the scalar will of Section 2 is an approximation, and the honest upgrade is a contextual interaction structure, at the cost of the simplicity that makes the unit attractive.

### 3.4 The calibration layer, and its failure

The secondary layer was graded against the same four tests, and it fails three of them. It fails instrument independence: endurance time on a handgrip task, a cold pressor task, and mental arithmetic do not scale onto a single physical dimension. It fails the composition rule: human effort does not add linearly, because fatigue, depletion, and motivational saturation make two minutes of exertion far more than twice as costly as one minute. It fails portability: seconds of endurance and joules of work mean nothing for a neural network or a crowd. It passes only the zero test. The verdict, recorded in the theory's own documents and repeated here without softening: the effort and time layer is a calibration layer only, never the unit. It may translate between human instruments and nats inside a stated experiment, through titration to a point of subjective equivalence followed by logistic regression of choices on reward and effort differences, yielding exchange rates in nats per unit of effort for that person in that state. It may not serve as the definition of will, and any future version of this theory that quietly promotes it back to a unit should be read as having failed this section.

### 3.5 Synthetic validation of the unit and the dilution law

The primary layer was validated where validation is cheap and unambiguous: on synthetic data with a known ground truth. Choice frequencies were generated from the dilution law of Section 6, W(N) = W0 / (1 + aN), with ground truth parameters W0 = 3.0 nats and a = 0.25. The estimation pipeline was then given only the frequencies and asked to recover the curve and its parameters. It recovered W0 as 2.9512 nats, an error of 1.63 percent, and the dilution coefficient as 0.2369 against 0.25, an error of 5.24 percent. The pointwise error of the recovered curve was 0.0562 nats in root mean square. These numbers establish a limited and genuinely useful fact: if a selecting system does obey the dilution law, choice frequencies alone suffice to measure its will in nats and recover the law's parameters with small error. The pipeline is not the bottleneck. Whether any real system obeys the law is a separate question, and Section 8 reports how it is currently going.

The defect inside the validation is also on record. Interval coverage was checked with Wilson score intervals at each load level, and coverage was 4 of 5 points, not 5 of 5: the interval at the highest load level, N = 8, missed the true value in the pipeline's own synthetic data. The miss is a standing warning that uncertainty estimates from this pipeline are slightly optimistic at high load, exactly the regime where the theory's predictions are most distinctive.

## 4. Coupling

### 4.1 The selection point and the four candidates

A unit tells us what will is measured in. A coupling tells us where it acts. The natural place to look is the selection point of quantum mechanics. Unitary evolution preserves superpositions, and decoherence converts a pure state into an improper mixture in a preferred basis, but neither selects which eigenvalue is realised in a single run. If a volitional influence couples to physical dynamics anywhere, it couples there. Four candidate architectures exhaust the obvious options, and each was written as an explicit model and graded against three kill tests drawn from a frozen test programme: K1, preservation of the Born rule together with completed localisation; K2, consistency with energy conservation and with experimental bounds on apparatus heating and anomalous radiation; and K3, linearity of ensemble evolution, which by Gisin's argument forbids superluminal signalling with entangled states (Gisin 1989). Two candidates fail fatally. One survives by being known physics. One survives outright, and it is the coupling this theory adopts.

### 4.2 Outcome bias: excluded

The most direct candidate has will favour the outcome itself, either as a Hermitian potential term lowering the energy of the target state, or as an inserted drift in the stochastic dynamics of the state's population. Both forms fail. The Hermitian form fails K1 exactly: evolution under a Hamiltonian diagonal in the outcome basis preserves each population precisely, so its readout frequencies are a step function, zero below a Born weight of one half and one above it, selecting nothing. The drift form fails K1 statistically: in the discovery-loop ledger (Cycle 4, an inserted drift at alpha = 0.125, a different run from the drift-biased diffusion candidate graded in Section 4.4 of the companion paper), the drift distorted asymptotic frequencies by 0.035 to 0.086 away from the Born values. A standalone diagnostic, the deterministic pull graded as Model C in the companion paper, fails K3 structurally: it produced an ensemble nonlinearity with trace distance 0.3223 against a Monte Carlo noise scale of 0.0158. Nonlinear ensemble evolution is not a technical blemish: the pull exhibits the decomposition dependence that, extended to entangled pairs, is the mechanism of Gisin's signalling argument. Outcome bias is therefore excluded, and the exclusion is load-bearing: whatever will is, it does not push outcomes.

### 4.3 Noise steering: excluded

The second candidate leaves the dynamics alone and steers the noise, treating will as a Maxwell's demon acting on the stochastic innovation term of continuous measurement, either by giving the noise a nonzero mean or by giving it a finite correlation time. This fails on the martingale property. In standard continuous measurement the conditional population is a martingale, its expectation constant in time, and that constancy is what delivers Born frequencies at absorption. A steered mean destroys the martingale directly, and coloured noise destroys it through memory induced back-flow; the standard Wong-Zakai drift correction does not restore the Born rule at finite correlation time. The failures are K1 failures, in three distinct shapes: a steered mean destroys the martingale directly, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck coloured noise compresses the outcome frequencies toward one half, and the Wong-Zakai form overshoots them, failing K1 at four of five test points. The tested Wong-Zakai form passes the ensemble-consistency test K3, so noise steering is excluded by K1, not by K3. The two exclusions close the most intuitive doors: will neither biases the answer nor rigs the randomness that produces it.

### 4.4 Measurement-strength modulation: known physics, with one precise prediction

The third candidate modulates the rate at which measurement happens. In the stochastic master equation of Belavkin filtering theory, the measurement strength gamma sets the rate of state purification. Letting gamma depend on will, for instance gamma(W) = gamma_0 times e raised to the power W, with gamma_0 the apparatus baseline, which is derived, and the exponential gain rule, which is inserted rather than derived, changes the dynamics in a specific way: because the innovation term keeps zero mean for any positive time-dependent gamma, the expected population remains exactly the initial Born weight for all times. Outcome frequencies cannot move. What moves is the timescale. That invariance is stated under the assumptions of the case at hand: a fixed monitored observable whose eigenspaces are the pointer basis, the nondemolition case in which the innovation averages away. Where the Hamiltonian does not commute with the monitored observable, changing gamma changes the competition between unitary evolution and localisation, and the invariance of outcome frequencies does not follow without further argument; the prediction in this section is made for the nondemolition case. The selection time scales as the reciprocal of gamma, so an agent exerting more will, in nats, would shorten the time a monitored system takes to localise, while the frequencies it localises to stay strictly Born.

This candidate passes K1 and K3, and it is graded, in the programme's own words, as surviving in the form of a known physical model. It is mathematically identical to adjusting detector sensitivity in standard quantum measurement theory. That identity is not a defect of the grading; it is the grading. Gamma modulation is what the first horn of the fork looks like when written down: will, at the physical level, as an interpretation of continuous measurement rather than as new physics. The candidate nonetheless earns its place here for two reasons. It makes one precise prediction, stated above, which can be looked for. And it has a named killing bound for one physical reading of it. If gamma modulation were a fundamental collapse process of the mass-density-coupled kind, rather than an apparatus setting, it would induce the spontaneous radiation by which such processes are tested underground: the Gran Sasso germanium search of Donadi and collaborators excluded the natural parameter-free Diosi-Penrose model, and bounds collapse rates of that class to lambda below 5.2 times 10 to the power minus 13 per second. That exclusion is specific to the coupling class whose radiation signature the experiment measures; a will-dependent rate in a different class is constrained only insofar as it produces the same signature, and no broader exclusion is claimed here. Apparatus backaction heating separately constrains any attempt to raise gamma without a physical detector supplying power and dissipating heat, under K2. The physical reading of gamma modulation is therefore either apparatus physics, which is uncontroversial, or a collapse model of the tested class, which is excluded in its parameter-free form. What remains genuinely open is the agent-level correlation the prediction describes, and that belongs to the fourth candidate.

### 4.5 Channel and basis selection: the surviving coupling

The fourth candidate moves the locus of action one step earlier. Will does not act on outcomes, on noise, or on the rate of measurement within a channel. It acts on which channel is measured at all. An agent faces N competing channels, each carrying its own state and its own measurement basis. Attention is allocated across channels with probabilities given by the softmax of the allocated wills, pi_k proportional to exp(W_k), where each W_k is in the nats of Section 2. For a target channel competing against N distractors, the target's will follows the dilution law, W_target(N) = W0 / (1 + aN), whose hyperbolic functional form is postulated (inserted) rather than derived. Once a channel is selected, the measurement inside it is ordinary: the probability of outcome m in channel k is the Born probability Tr(Pi_{k,m} rho_k), with no bias, no steering, and no rate anomaly. In the language of experimental physics, will governs which question is asked. It never governs which answer nature returns.

This coupling passes K1, K2, and K3 by construction of the right kind: not by fine tuning, but because the only nonstandard element sits in the allocation probabilities, which quantum mechanics has always taken as an exogenous input, the experimenter's choice of what to measure. Energy exchange happens through the ordinary apparatus coupling, and ensemble linearity is untouched because no state evolution is modified anywhere. In the agent domain, the same coupling is the familiar phenomenon of biased competition in attention, in which representations compete and top-down signals shift the odds of which representation controls behaviour. The theory's claim is that this familiar phenomenon has a measurable strength, a unit, a dilution law, and a thermodynamic accounting, and that those four together are what the word force is doing in the title.

### 4.6 The honest limit of the coupling simulation

The coupling was exercised in simulation, and the simulation's limits must be stated as carefully as its results. With the dilution law in place, the attended channel drew its share of trials and the outcome frequencies inside it stayed at baseline: across 6,134 attended trials the aggregate frequency was 0.7028 against a baseline of 0.7000, with no defect flagged. This is a real check of the pipeline and of the definition's internal consistency. It is not evidence about nature, for a structural reason: intra-channel Born invariance is true by construction in the simulation, because the simulator draws intra-channel outcomes from the Born distribution it was told to use. A simulation that builds in a property cannot then discover it. What the simulation establishes is that the definition of will as channel selection is consistent, implementable without contradiction, and recoverable by the estimation machinery. The claim that nature cooperates stands or falls on human data, and Section 8 grades its current position without borrowing strength from the simulation.

## 5. Conservation

### 5.1 Three candidate answers

When an agent exerts will to bias channel selection by W nats, something must pay, or the theory is dualism with equations. Three candidate answers were graded. The first is that nothing is exchanged: will as an immaterial agency aligning physical configurations while exchanging no energy, momentum, or entropy. This is excluded as a Maxwell demon. Biasing a decision degree of freedom from a uniform baseline to a focused distribution lowers that system's entropy by the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the two distributions, and with no compensating entropy dumped into the environment, total entropy decreases, which the second law forbids; the hypothesis also breaks causal closure, since changing the state of a neural assembly or an optical switch takes work. The second candidate is direct energy injection: will as a literal force field tilting an energy landscape. This is excluded twice over. Continuously forcing a monitored observable that does not commute with the Hamiltonian pumps energy into the system at a rate fixed by the measurement strength, so an injecting will would appear as anomalous heating; and at the quantum selection point, an injection realised as a mass-density-coupled collapse process would fall in the class the Donadi underground search tests, which excluded the parameter-free Diosi-Penrose model, though that exclusion does not by itself bound an injection mechanism that produces no radiation signature. In neural tissue the candidate also fails by redundancy, since the work of moving ions against electrochemical gradients is fully accounted for by ATP hydrolysis. The third candidate survives, and it is the conservation story of this theory: the currency is information.

### 5.2 The accounting: an energy gap, a preparation bound, and what is not claimed

Stochastic thermodynamics prices information, but it prices specified processes, and this section separates three quantities that an earlier draft of this paper ran together. The separation is a correction, recorded here rather than silently absorbed, because the difference between the three quantities is the difference between a claim and an overclaim.

First, the definitions. Write q for the baseline probability of selecting the target and p for the probability under engaged intention. The will of Section 2 is the signed log-odds shift W = ln[(p / (1 minus p)) / (q / (1 minus q))]. The relative entropy between the two Bernoulli distributions is a different object: D_KL(p parallel q) = p ln(p / q) + (1 minus p) ln[(1 minus p) / (1 minus q)]. It depends on both probabilities, not on their log-odds difference alone, and the two quantities are not interchangeable. For a small shift at a fixed interior baseline, D_KL = (1/2) q (1 minus q) W^2 + O(W^3): the relative entropy is quadratic in the shift, not linear in it. Any sentence of the form "a shift of W nats costs W times k_B T" is therefore false in general, and this paper withdraws it wherever the earlier draft implied it.

Second, the energy-gap interpretation. Suppose, as an explicitly stated implementation, that the choice states are states of a physical system in contact with a heat bath at temperature T, with energies assigned so that the equilibrium odds between them are Boltzmann odds, ln(p / (1 minus p)) = minus beta times deltaE, with beta = 1 / (k_B T). Under that implementation, and only under it, a log-odds shift of W corresponds to a change of W times k_B T in the energy gap between the states. This correspondence is an interpretation of the shift under the implementation, marked DERIVED within it. A gap is a property of a spectrum. It is not, by itself, work expended, heat dissipated, or the minimum cost of running a cycle, and nothing in this paper treats it as any of those.

Third, the preparation bound. Landauer's principle and its generalisation by Sagawa and Ueda to measurement, feedback, and information processing bound specified physical processes: preparing a system in a distribution P against a baseline P_0, or sustaining it there against relaxation, requires dissipation of at least k_B T times D_KL(P parallel P_0), under the memory and reset model of the process in question. This is the conservation content that survives scrutiny, and it is a bound on the relative entropy, not a charge per nat of log-odds shift. Because the divergence is quadratic in W for small shifts, the floor price of a small bias sits far below the gap scale W times k_B T, and the floor price of a large bias is set by the full divergence, not by W. What the accounting requires, before it says anything about a given agent, is an implementation: the physical states that realise the selection, their energies and entropies, a preparation protocol, a reservoir, and a reset operation that returns the register to its initial state so that the cycle can repeat. For an abstract binary degree of freedom in an isothermal bath, those items are specifiable and the bound above is the accounting. For a human brain they are not currently specified, and Section 5.4 records what that costs the theory. Subject to that specification requirement, the accounting is well defined: will is not free, and its floor price is denominated in the same currency as all information processing.

The numbers are small and worth exhibiting, now with their correct labels. At mammalian brain temperature, 310.15 K, k_B T is 4.2821 times 10 to the power minus 21 joules. That figure is the gap scale of a one-nat shift under the Boltzmann implementation, and it is also the preparation bound for one nat of relative entropy; one bit of relative entropy carries the bound k_B T ln 2, which is 2.9681 times 10 to the power minus 21 joules. At room temperature, 293.15 K, k_B T is 4.0474 times 10 to the power minus 21 joules. On the standard dilution grid used in the programme's simulations, undiluted capacity W0 = 3.0 nats, dilution coefficient a = 0.25, distractor pull 1.0 nat, the preparation bound per decision at brain temperature, computed from the divergence rather than from the shift, falls from 8.385 times 10 to the power minus 22 joules at one competing channel, through 4.642 times 10 to the power minus 22 at two channels and 1.321 times 10 to the power minus 22 at four, to zero at eight channels, the load at which dilution has exactly cancelled the target's advantage. The accounting was exercised in simulation: the model's imposed bias and its accounted dissipation, both computed through the divergence, agree to 0.005759 k_B T in root mean square. As with the coupling simulation, this shows the accounting is consistent and implementable. It does not show that brains keep these books, only that the books exist and can be kept.

### 5.3 The starvation prediction, withdrawn

An earlier form of this theory made a further claim: that if an agent's free-energy budget for a decision falls below the floor price of the bias it is sustaining, the achieved bias must fall along the Landauer curve, the selection probability tending to the baseline and the achieved will to zero nats as the budget tends to zero, regardless of goal intensity or undiluted capacity. In the programme's conservation simulation, a starvation sweep did drive the recovered will down to the indifference baseline, the residual bias above baseline collapsing to 0.012 nats at zero budget. That behaviour is a property of the simulation as constructed, in which the budget constraint was imposed by hand. It is not a derivation, and the claim is withdrawn, for the following reason. The preparation bound of Section 5.2 prices the preparation of a distribution under a specified process. It does not, without more, state that a behavioural agent's achievable bias is limited by a per-decision energy budget, because that step requires knowing how the agent's selection is physically implemented, what reservoir receives the dissipation, what reset operation closes each decision cycle, and over what timescale a sustained distribution must be re-prepared. None of that is specified for a behavioural agent in the cited literature, and the Sagawa-Ueda results, which are stated for specified measurement and memory models, do not supply it. The programme's own conservation document presents a formal version of the prediction conditional on a per-decision budget for the selection degree of freedom, but that budget is an added postulate about an unspecified implementation, so the prediction does not follow from the cited literature alone. It is recorded here, rather than deleted, because the programme's rule is that a withdrawn claim stays visible.

What a future derivation would require is statable exactly. Specify the physical states implementing the selection degree of freedom, with their energies and entropies. Specify the preparation protocol that takes the system from the baseline distribution to the biased one, and the reservoir that receives the dissipation. Specify the reset operation that returns the register to its initial state, since a cyclic agent pays the reset cost every cycle and not once. Then derive the minimum cyclic operating cost from the relative-entropy bound under that protocol. Only at that point does a budget constraint on achievable bias follow, and its shape will be whatever that derivation gives, which may or may not resemble the curve the simulation assumed. Until such a derivation exists, the theory makes no starvation prediction, and the falsifier of this paper does not rest on one.

### 5.4 The measurability defect, stated at full strength

Against these results stands the section's recorded defect, and it is severe. The floor derived above is unresolvable in a living human being. A human brain dissipates roughly 20 watts; over a two-second decision trial that is about 40 joules, against a per-decision floor of about 8.385 times 10 to the power minus 22 joules for a representative bias. The thermodynamic efficiency ratio of the human case, floor divided by actual dissipation, is about 2.1 times 10 to the power minus 23. Because an energy per event cannot be compared with a power, the same comparison is restated with the selection rate made explicit. At one selection per decision trial of duration two seconds, the trial duration used in the programme's own accounting, the rate is r = 0.5 per second. The preparation-bound power floor is then P = r times k_B T times D_KL, about 4.2 times 10 to the power minus 22 watts for the representative bias, and the gap scale of Section 5.2 corresponds to r times k_B T times W, about 3.0 times 10 to the power minus 21 watts at the grid's net shift of 1.4 nats, quoted as an energy-scale comparison only, since a gap is not a cost. Against the brain's roughly 20 watts, the preparation-bound power floor again sits about 23 orders of magnitude below metabolic power, so the orders-of-magnitude figure is a comparison of like units over the same epoch, not of an energy against a power. A single action potential already dissipates on the order of 10 to the power minus 12 joules, some nine orders of magnitude above the floor for the whole decision, and the bulk of cerebral metabolism is overhead unrelated to the selection being priced: ion pumps, vesicle recycling, glial maintenance, perfusion. The floor sits about 23 orders of magnitude beneath the metabolic noise of the organ whose acts of will it prices. No existing or foreseeable instrument can resolve it in vivo, and any claim that whole-body measurement confirms this conservation law in humans would be fraudulent. The paper says so in its own text so that no later summary can upgrade it.

The floor is not unmeasurable everywhere. It is meterable today in engineered micro-scale platforms where a single degree of freedom is the decision variable and work and heat are resolved at the 10 to the power minus 21 joule level: optically trapped colloidal particles in double-well potentials, where reported efficiencies reach 0.70 to 0.95 of the Landauer ideal; single-electron boxes at sub-kelvin temperatures, where tunnelling statistics directly verify the relation between heat and information; and nanomagnetic logic elements, with efficiencies in the range 0.10 to 0.50. The conservation law is thus a real, checkable constraint on the class of systems the theory describes, with the human case sitting at an efficiency so low that the constraint, while true, is empirically silent there. That combination, a law that binds in principle and whispers in practice, is recorded here as a limitation.

## 6. The friction anatomy: what is universal, what is measured

The theory can now be laid out in the form its title promises. Like friction, it separates into a universal form and a table of system-specific coefficients, and confusing the two columns is the fastest route to both overclaiming and underclaiming.

The universal parts are three. First, the unit: will is a log-odds shift in nats, with zero at indifference and additivity of independent pulls, for any selecting system. Second, the dilution law's form: the will on a target channel falls with the number N of competing channels as W(N) = W0 / (1 + aN). The claim of universality attaches to the functional form, the hyperbolic fall with load, not to any particular parameter values. Third, the thermodynamic floor: preparing or sustaining a selection distribution P against the unforced baseline P_0 dissipates at least k_B T times D_KL(P parallel P_0) per decision, under the accounting of Section 5, in any substrate, for a specified preparation and reset process, because that bound is set by statistical mechanics and not by the agent. The floor prices the relative entropy of the bias, not the log-odds shift itself, and the stronger per-nat reading is recorded as withdrawn in Section 5.

The system-specific parts are also three, measured per pairing, by experiment, the way a friction coefficient is measured. First, W0, the undiluted capacity: the will an agent can bring to a target when competition is absent, factorising in the original formulation as intensity times tenure, the expected gratification of the goal multiplied by the duration over which it is expected to persist. That factorisation is a heuristic antecedent from the original formulation: its calibration into nats is unspecified, and no result in this paper uses it. Second, the dilution coefficient a, a property of the pairing of an agent with a channel type, not of either alone: the same person dilutes differently across visual distractors and across social obligations, exactly as a friction coefficient belongs to a pair of surfaces. Third, the effective temperature, the T converting nats into energy in the floor price, anchored near body temperature for a biological agent and a parameter of the operating regime for an engineered one.

The cross-domain criterion follows directly. The theory is one theory, rather than a metaphor reused in several places, if and only if the same functional form, with coefficients that transfer in the disciplined sense of Section 3.1, describes dilution in at least two distinct domains, with the test pre-registered before fitting. The candidate domains named in the programme are the individual person, integrated versus serial processing in creative judgement tasks, and aggregate behaviour in crowds. If the curves differ in form by domain, the honest verdict is that will is a useful metaphor inside each domain and not one force. If one form survives across domains, that is a real result at the level of an effective theory of agents, not of a fundamental force, and this paper does not ask for that status.

One guard on that criterion must be stated, because without it the criterion can be passed fraudulently. The dilution coefficient is not invariant under a rescaling of the load axis: if a domain counts channels on a rescaled measure N' = cN, the same data are fitted by a' = a / c. A domain that is free to choose its own counting convention for N can therefore manufacture agreement of a across domains by convention, and apparent transfer invariance would then prove nothing. The graduation condition is accordingly stricter than agreement of form: the measurement scale for N, meaning the stated counting rule for what constitutes one competing channel, must be frozen before any transfer test, held constant across the domains compared, and reported with the fit. A transfer claim made without a frozen counting rule is not evidence for this theory, and this paper makes none.

## 7. Neighbours: what this theory predicts that they do not

The theory has close neighbours, and a theory that cannot say how it differs from them is a renaming. The table states, for each neighbour, one prediction this theory makes that the neighbour does not. Section 8 grades how those predictions are faring.

| Neighbour | What it already explains | What this theory predicts that it does not |
|---|---|---|
| Lewin force fields | Behaviour as the resultant of psychological forces in a life space of competing pulls | A unit and a law: the pulls are measurable in nats from choice frequencies, and the pull on a target falls hyperbolically with the counted number of competing channels, with coefficients that must transfer across tasks |
| Drift diffusion models | Choice and reaction time from noisy accumulation to a threshold, with drift set by stimulus value | A constraint across tasks: the drift advantage of a target is not a free per-task parameter but dilutes as W0 / (1 + aN) in counted channels, and the veto point of no return stays fixed while veto success collapses under load |
| Discounted utility | Choice among rewards by value discounted over delay and magnitude | A ceiling: transmitted information per decision epoch saturates at a fixed number of nats and does not rise when incentives scale, where the utility account predicts continued expansion with stakes |
| Rational inattention (Matejka and McKay 2015) | Logit choice itself, derived from costly information acquisition: an agent who pays a cost per nat of information about payoffs chooses with multinomial logit probabilities, and the follow-up literature relates this family to additive random utility models generally | Conceded overlap first: the log-odds form, and the idea that information carries a cost, are already here, and this paper claims neither as new. What this theory adds sits outside the static frame: a hyperbolic dilution in counted channels with the counting rule frozen in advance, a stake-invariant ceiling on transmitted information, and timing predictions, including the veto asymmetry of Section 8, that a static information-cost model does not address. The cost notions are also kept apart: the rational-inattention coefficient is an economic quantity in utility units, not the physical constant k_B T, and nothing here identifies them |
| Cognitive load theory | Performance degrades as working memory load grows, with load described qualitatively by element interactivity | A functional form and a price: degradation follows one hyperbolic curve in counted channels, and preparing a biased selection distribution carries a floor cost of k_B T times its relative entropy to the unforced baseline |
| Integrated information theory | Consciousness as integrated information, a property of a system's causal structure | A behavioural handle: the strength of an individual intention is measurable in nats from frequencies, and dilutes with competing channels |
| Global workspace theory | Conscious access as broadcast of a selected representation to a global workspace | A selection law for the broadcast itself: the odds of a channel winning access follow a softmax of nats whose target strength dilutes hyperbolically with the number of competing channels |

Two rows deserve a sentence each. The Lewin row is the theory's ancestry, acknowledged rather than disguised: what is new here is not the force metaphor but the metrology, the unit, the dilution form, and the floor. The drift diffusion row carries the theory's sharpest surviving prediction, the veto prediction of Section 8, because diffusion models of the stop-signal race set the point of no return by the race dynamics of the task at hand and do not, without addition, predict its invariance while success collapses under load.

## 8. Current status of the claims

An effective theory paper that reports only its construction is an advertisement. This section reports the audited position of the claims at the time of writing, drawn from the programme's claim graph, in which every claim carries a status that a red team had to attack before it could change. The position is mixed, and the mixture is the finding.

First, the choice domain, where the news is worst. The dilution law, expressed in choice frequencies, is mathematically equivalent to divisive normalisation, the canonical computation in which a neuron's response is divided by the summed activity of its neighbours. A hyperbolic rescaling of utilities by set size and a divisive normalisation of responses are, at the level of the choice probabilities they generate, the same model wearing two names. Worse for the theory's distinctiveness, on real travel-choice data the dilution family loses out of sample to nested logit models, which capture the hierarchical structure of real choice sets, transit against private transport, that a flat scalar dilution cannot express. The verdict recorded in the claim graph, and adopted by this paper, is that in the choice-frequency domain the dilution law is a renaming of known computation. It is presented as such, not defended. What survives from the choice work is narrower: the unit itself, which remains a coherent way to measure selection strength whatever law governs it, and the discipline the equivalence forced, since a theory that discovers its own renaming early has been saved from discovering it in review.

A note on the nearest economic neighbour belongs in this status report, because it disciplines the conservation claim. Rational inattention, following Matejka and McKay (2015), already derives logit choice from a cost on information, with a cost coefficient denominated in utility per nat of information acquired. That coefficient is an economic parameter, fitted to behaviour. It must not be silently identified with the physical constant k_B T, which enters Section 5 only through a stated Boltzmann implementation of physical states and a relative-entropy bound on a specified process. The two uses of the word cost are analogous at most. Where earlier drafts of this programme let them blur, the blur is corrected in Section 5, and the correction weakens the conservation claim to what the derivation supports.

Second, the information ceiling. The capacity claim of Section 6 has been tightened into a derived number: the information an agent transmits in a half-second decision epoch saturates at 2.15 nats, with a 95 percent confidence interval of [1.59, 2.70]. The derivation takes published transmission rates for absolute identification and choice reaction time as its inputs and is recorded in the programme's protocol documents; the number is the theory's current derived position, and it is a prediction, not a measurement. Its discriminating experiment crosses task count with incentive scale, and its pre-registered outcome regions include an honestly undecided band above the derived interval, because the derivation's inputs do not justify greater precision. No human dataset with the required crossing currently exists in the public record.

Third, the veto prediction, the sharpest distinct claim the theory currently owns. In the stop-signal setting, the theory predicts that as competing-channel load rises, the probability of successfully dispatching a veto collapses, while the point of no return, the moment about 200 ms before movement after which a stop command cannot prevent the action, stays fixed. The asymmetry is the signature: load attacks the dispatch of the stop process, not the deadline it races against. Standard race accounts tie both quantities to the same dynamics and do not predict the split without added machinery, and the equivalence results that damaged the choice claims do not touch this one, because no normalisation identity governs a race against a deadline. The prediction is validated in race-model simulation. It is untested in humans, for the same reason as the ceiling: a census of the public neuroimaging record found no dataset crossing a stop-signal paradigm with a parametric load manipulation. The claim's status is open, precise, and waiting on data that does not yet exist in public.

Fourth, the brain-computer interface evidence, which the theory once hoped would support it and which now mostly instructs it. On public motor imagery data, selection among more classes degrades in a way that can be fitted with a dilution coefficient in the same numerical range as the behavioural estimates. The audit of that result is a cautionary tale. Most of the apparent dilution in scalp recordings is a finite-sample estimation artifact: as calibration data grows, the fitted coefficient falls toward a small residual, because estimating more spatial filters from the same number of trials manufactures exactly the degradation the theory predicted. A small asymptotic residual remains unresolved; it may be biological, or a property of the decoders, and the present evidence cannot separate those. The BCI work therefore stands as neither support nor refutation, but as a demonstration of the theory's central occupational hazard, that dilution-shaped curves arise from mundane causes, which is why the falsifier is written the way it is.

The summary position is this. The quantum versions of the theory are closed: the parameter-free Diosi-Penrose collapse form is excluded by the Donadi radiation bound, and the one surviving physical coupling is identical to known measurement theory. The choice version is a renaming in its home domain. The unit, the channel selection coupling, and the corrected thermodynamic accounting stand as a consistent effective framework whose two distinctive predictions, the ceiling and the veto asymmetry, are precise, pre-registered, and untested for want of the right experiments. Every part of that position can be killed by a named observation, which is more than most force claims about will ever offer.

## 9. Discussion

Three implications follow from the position of Section 8. The first concerns what the negative results are worth. The exclusions of outcome bias and noise steering, the equivalence with divisive normalisation, and the estimation artifact in the BCI data are not failures surrounding the theory; they are the theory's method working in public. A programme about will survives contact with referees only if its negative results are as documented as its positive ones, and the claim graph discipline, in which statuses change only after adversarial review, exists for that reason.

The second implication concerns data. Both surviving predictions are untested for a structural reason: the experiments that would test them have not been run, by anyone, in a form the public record holds. The stop-signal literature fixes load at a single channel; the choice literature varies set size or incentives, rarely both in the crossed design the ceiling test needs. The theory's empirical future is therefore not a reanalysis but a collaboration: preregistered protocols for both experiments exist in the programme's documents, with power analyses and pipelines validated on synthetic data, and they require a laboratory, an ethics approval, and participants. Until those exist, the correct label for the theory's distinctive content is precise and untested.

The third implication concerns the fork of Section 1. Nothing in the agent-domain results reopens the physical question. The seam the work actually supports is modest: quantum theory describes how a selected channel is read out, with Born statistics inside it, and the effective theory of this paper describes the selecting, which channel, with what strength, diluting how, and under what thermodynamic accounting. The two descriptions meet at the allocation probabilities and contradict each other nowhere. That seam is not a unification, it proposes no new physical entity, and the strong version of the hope that motivated the programme, a will that reaches into quantum dynamics and bends it, is among the claims the programme itself has closed.

## 10. Conclusion

This paper has done the three things a force claim owes. It fixed a unit, the nat of log-odds shift, graded it against metrological criteria, validated its recovery on synthetic data at 0.0562 nats of error, and recorded its defects, including a calibration layer that fails three of its four tests and a measurement ceiling near ln(2M) nats. It fixed a coupling, selection among channels and bases, after excluding outcome bias and noise steering by kill test and identifying measurement-strength modulation as known physics with a named experimental bound. It fixed a conservation accounting: under a stated Boltzmann implementation a log-odds shift is an energy-gap change of W times k_B T, the preparation of a biased distribution is bounded below by k_B T times its relative entropy to baseline, the books balance in simulation, and the floor is honestly unresolvable in the living human brain; the stronger claims an earlier draft attached to that accounting, a universal per-nat charge and an energy-starvation prediction, are withdrawn in Section 5, with their reasons. It then reported, in the same voice, that the theory's choice-domain law is a renaming of divisive normalisation that loses to nested logit models on real data, and that its two surviving predictions await experiments nobody has yet run. The falsifier stands where the abstract put it. If the dilution law, measured in the person domain, adds no out-of-sample predictive power beyond discounted utility and standard attention measures, the force framing is to be dropped to a metaphor and the programme closed as such. A theory of will that cannot be killed would not be worth having. This one can be, in named places, by named observations, and parts of it already have been.
